# 03. Fixation and Blue residuals

This memo treats **fixation** and **Blue residuals** in HSS as observation targets within a connection structure.

Fixation is observed as a state in which a symbol or meaning closes into a single interpretation, role, evaluation, or reaction.

Blue residuals are a state in which unresolved interpretive room, discomfort, traces, or reconnection possibility remain.

Blue residuals are not every state that is simply not fixated.

Blue residuals remain when something is not fully absorbed into processing forms or short-cycle reactions and can later return to another context.

## Fixation

A fixed symbol may appear in the following ways.

- It can be shared at high speed.
- It can be reused easily.
- It can circulate at low cost.
- It can close into a single role, evaluation, reaction, or interpretation.

At the same time, it may also appear in the following ways.

- The connection area may become narrower.
- Reinterpretation may become less likely to occur.
- It may become harder to expand into different connections.
- Routes back to original context, value, or memory may become thinner.

### Observation conditions

Fixation is easier to observe when:

- a symbol is repeatedly used with almost the same meaning or reaction;
- a short label, score, ranking, role, or category becomes enough to process the target;
- alternative interpretations become harder to express;
- the processing form circulates faster than the context behind it;
- the route back to layered history, purpose, or original value becomes weaker.

### Weak conditions

Fixation is weak as an HSS observation when:

- the symbol remains open to multiple interpretations;
- the processing form does not narrow reconnection routes;
- the same form supports richer return to context;
- there is no observable change in circulation, interpretation, or routing;
- ordinary explanation describes the case without needing HSS vocabulary.

### Counterexamples / difficult cases

- A fixed form may deepen reinterpretation, as in haiku, rakugo, or fixed performance forms.
- A shared label may help people preserve a route to context rather than close it.
- A stable evaluation category may make previously invisible value visible.

## Blue residuals

Blue residuals are observed as unabsorbed room that remains after processing, reaction, or circulation.

They are not defined by HSS as “good” in themselves.

However, from the author’s standpoint, they are treated as an important auxiliary line because they may support layered history and re-expansion.

### Observation basis

Blue residuals are not judged by an absolute threshold.

Similar to how buzz cannot be defined only by “more than N views,” Blue residuals are observed through a combination of signs.

For example, Blue residuals are easier to observe when:

- something is referenced again later in another time, scene, or context;
- it does not end with its first meaning or reaction, and connects to another question, creative act, judgment, or discomfort;
- it leaves room not fully explainable by a single processing form such as KPI, view count, buzz, price, evaluation, or classification;
- questions remain, such as “What was that?”, “Why does it bother me?”, or “Where can it connect?”;
- imitation, quotation, transformation, reinterpretation, different use, or transfer to another field occurs;
- even without a clear conclusion or output, it remains as memory, discomfort, memo, conversation, revisit, save, or re-reference.

These are not proofs. They are observation signs.

When these signs are weak, HSS does not treat the target strongly as Blue residuals.

### Difference from fixation

Fixation is observed as a state in which a symbol or meaning closes into a single interpretation, role, evaluation, or reaction.

Blue residuals are observed as a state in which, even after processing, room remains for another interpretation, question, context, or reconnection.

In short, fixation closes; Blue residuals leave unabsorbed room.

### Weak conditions

Blue residuals are weak as an HSS observation when:

- there is no later re-reference, memory, discomfort, revisit, or reinterpretation;
- the target is simply unclear, vague, or unfinished without any observable reconnection possibility;
- the ambiguity mainly produces confusion, exploitation, or responsibility avoidance;
- the supposed residual room is fully absorbed into a processing form;
- HSS vocabulary adds no difference to an ordinary description.

### Counterexamples / difficult cases

- Ambiguity may weaken responsibility rather than support creativity.
- Unresolved room may be exploited as branding, mystique, or evasion.
- A short-cycle market or short video may still generate later layered history.

## Reconnectable difference

A reconnectable difference is a difference in change that generates new connections or reinterpretations while maintaining an existing connection area.

Rather than completely breaking the connection, small differences such as the following may lead to re-expansion.

- Slightly departing from expectations.
- Slightly shifting meaning.
- Leaving a slight sense of discomfort.

### Observation conditions

Reconnectable difference is easier to observe when:

- the difference is noticeable but does not cut off the existing connection area;
- the difference causes reinterpretation, conversation, imitation, creative response, or changed behavior;
- earlier memory or context can still be called back;
- a new route appears without erasing the older route.

### Weak conditions and difficult cases

- If the difference is too large and the connection area breaks, it may not be reconnectable.
- If the difference is too small to change interpretation or routing, HSS observation is weak.
- If the difference is only novelty for attention, it may not support layered history.

## Layered history

Layered history is a record or history formed as different connections and Flows multiply and become layered through continuous connection.

### Observation conditions

Layered history is easier to observe when:

- repeated contact remains as memory, context, habit, relationship, record, or shared reference;
- later events can call back earlier contexts;
- compression into a symbol still leaves a route back to accumulated context;
- continuity can be traced across time, scenes, people, or processing forms.

### Weak conditions and difficult cases

- If no accumulation across time can be shown, the observation is weak.
- Layered history may produce rigidity, exclusion, or in-group closure rather than re-expansion.
- Long duration alone does not establish layered history if no reconnection route can be shown.

## Feedback deficit

Feedback deficit is a state in which negative feedback or corrective action that would normally be expected to occur becomes weakened, delayed, or difficult to observe.

### Observation conditions

Feedback deficit is easier to observe when:

- positive feedback, rewards, approval, or indicators remain strong;
- discomfort, fatigue, reassessment, or distancing becomes hard to express or notice;
- platform indicators, recognition, or short-term rewards make correction less likely;
- the system keeps circulating even when connection routes or layered history become thinner.

### Weak conditions and difficult cases

- If correction is visible and changes routing, feedback deficit is weak.
- If only dissatisfaction is observed without a structural routing change, HSS observation is weak.
- Positive feedback may coexist with strong correction in some communities or organizations.

## Objective function capture

Objective function capture is a state in which what an individual or organization believed to be its own objective is reshaped according to the objective function of an external system, such as a platform, evaluation system, market indicator, or similar structure.

### Observation conditions

Objective function capture is easier to observe when:

- indicators, rankings, KPIs, prices, view counts, or evaluations become objective-like;
- original value, purpose, or context becomes harder to revisit;
- routing initiative appears to move outward toward the external system;
- actions are optimized for the processing form rather than the original route.

### Weak conditions and difficult cases

- Indicators can make previously invisible value visible.
- Evaluation systems can help preserve accountability or shared attention.
- If the original purpose remains reconnectable and can revise the indicator, capture is weak.

## Relationship to Cultural Formation

In HSS, cultural formation is provisionally placed as a process in which connections and Flow accumulate over time.

For that reason, the following balance is treated as an observation target.

- High-speed circulation of fixed symbols.
- Reconnectability through Blue residuals.
- Reconnectable difference.
- Formation of layered history / stratified history.

## Treatment

This memo is not intended to fully define fixation or Blue residuals.

It is a light observational note for organizing how, within connection structures, fixed things, remaining room, and reconnected things may appear to circulate.
