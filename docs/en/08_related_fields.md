# 08. Related fields

HSS was not constructed by referring to the theories, studies, or papers listed below.

The initial concepts, core vocabulary, and observation axes of HSS were not designed based on these fields.

After HSS had been formed as an observation model, we noticed that some existing fields have partially similar concerns or can serve as auxiliary connection points.

This document does not present sources, foundations, or justifications for HSS.

## Formation order note

In the formation order of HSS, the initial vocabulary and observation axes were first formed through creative work, operations, organizations, culture, and dialogue with AI.

After that, existing fields were compared with HSS, and fields with nearby concerns were organized in this document.

For that reason, the fields listed here are not the starting point of HSS. They are treated as reference points that may help readers connect HSS to existing domains after HSS had already been formed.

HSS is not built from these fields.

HSS does not integrate, replace, or supersede them.

HSS uses them as auxiliary lines for readers.

## Core module and related fields

The HSS core module does not dismiss the fact that the flow may look common.

It treats that common flow as a minimum pattern of connection structure that repeatedly appears across multiple domains.

This concern partially connects with fields such as story grammar, semiotics, social systems, cultural memory, cultural evolution, indicator optimization, sensemaking, attention economy, affordance, and Self-Determination Theory.

However, HSS does not integrate, replace, or stand above those fields.

What HSS observes is not a theory of a specific domain, but whether something layers, what it is compressed into, where reconnection or re-expansion occurs, and where fixation or narrowing of reconnection routes occurs across culture, markets, organizations, platforms, evaluation systems, and symbol circulation.

## HSS-specific structural limitations

HSS can coarsely observe connection structures that repeatedly appear across culture, creative work, markets, organizations, platforms, evaluation systems, and symbol circulation.

At the same time, this cross-domain stance creates HSS-specific structural limitations.

- A single core module can over-simplify situations where parallel and competing connection routes run at the same time.
- Because HSS centers symbols, processing forms, and reconnection routes, emotion, embodiment, desire, and pleasure/discomfort are not handled directly.
- Because HSS reads across domains, it is less precise than specialized theories for institutional, historical, psychological, economic, or technical details.
- If ordinary explanation and HSS explanation do not differ, it may look like after-the-fact relabeling.
- Because HSS observes through combinations of signs more than quantitative thresholds, observation includes interpretive discretion.
- Being readable through HSS vocabulary does not mean HSS observation is strong. Additional observation points such as conversion into processing forms, changes in reconnection routes, fixation, Blue residuals, feedback deficit, and objective function capture are needed.

These are not defects for simply rejecting HSS. They are boundaries for preventing over-application.

HSS is not a replacement for related fields. It is treated as an auxiliary observation frame for coarsely observing repeated connection structures.

## Individual notes

The following notes do not explain every field in the comparison table at the same grain size.

They briefly organize representative fields that may help HSS readers connect to existing domains.

### Semiotics

The HSS term `symbol` may partially look close to discussions in semiotics, such as sign, object, and interpretant.

Semiotics is one relatively strong connection point for thinking about cycles near sign, object, and interpretant.

However, HSS was not constructed by referring to semiotics, and it does not define semiotic meaning itself.

HSS observes whether a symbol can reconnect to context, relations, layered history, and routing.

### Goodhart's Law / indicator optimization

Goodhart's Law is widely known as a problem in which a measure becomes less able to preserve what it was meant to measure once it becomes a target.

HSS was not constructed by referring to Goodhart's Law.

However, HSS terms such as compressed symbol, KPI, evaluation indicator, and objective function capture have partially similar concerns when observing how indicators can narrow reconnection routes to the original purpose or value.

HSS observes how higher-level purposes, questions, and values are compressed into indicators, and how the possibility of returning to the original route may become narrower afterward.

### Boundary Object

Boundary Object is often discussed as an object that can be shared for cooperation while different communities may interpret it differently.

Boundary Object can be useful when reading invoice bundles, documents, tickets, labels, KPIs, and source anchors as objects crossing multiple social worlds or processing contexts.

HSS was not constructed by referring to boundary object theory.

However, HSS may observe source anchors, invoices, tickets, specifications, business cases, approval forms, KPIs, and labels as processing forms that cross multiple connection areas.

HSS observes whether such objects preserve connection possibility, circulate only as processing forms, or make reconnection routes thicker or narrower.

### Sensemaking

Sensemaking is known as an area that deals with how ambiguous situations or experiences become meaningful within organizations or among people.

Sensemaking can be a useful connection point when considering organizational meaning formation.

HSS was not constructed by referring to sensemaking research.

HSS also does not define meaning-making as a psychological process.

HSS observes which symbols, records, labels, and processing forms can return to which contexts or connection routes.

### Affordance / connection possibility

Affordance is related to the question of what possibilities for action an environment or object offers to an agent.

Affordance can help consider possibilities that arise among object, environment, and receiver.

HSS was not constructed by referring to affordance theory.

However, HSS focuses on reconnectable contexts and symbol routing rather than properties of the object alone.

HSS observes not only physical possibilities for action, but also whether symbols, records, invoices, KPIs, expressions, and source anchors can connect to contexts, memories, organizational processing, cultural reuse, or later reconnection.

### Self-Determination Theory

Self-Determination Theory is known as a psychological theory of motivation that discusses autonomy, competence, and relatedness.

This connection is a limited auxiliary line.

HSS was not constructed by referring to Self-Determination Theory.

HSS also does not define motivation, desire, autonomy, competence, relatedness, or psychological needs.

However, there may be a partial connection in observing how what is called self-determination, autonomy, self-responsibility, or self-control is placed into connection routes or processing forms.

## Caution

The fields listed here are not sources, foundations, or justifications for HSS.

HSS does not integrate, replace, or stand above these theories.

HSS is a working observation model for observing connection, compression, reconnection, layered history, Shiwa, and Blue residuals.

This document is placed as an auxiliary line for readers who want to connect HSS to existing domains.
